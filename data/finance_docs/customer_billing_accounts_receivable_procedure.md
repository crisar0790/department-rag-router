
# Customer Billing and Accounts Receivable Procedure

Document ID: FIN-006
Department: Finance
Version: 1.0
Effective date: 2026-01-01
Company: AR Cloud
Document type: Synthetic procedure for an educational project

## Purpose and scope

AR Cloud maintains a structured customer billing and accounts receivable process to ensure that customers are invoiced accurately, payments are recorded correctly, and outstanding balances are monitored consistently.

This procedure establishes the responsibilities and controls associated with customer billing, invoice issuance, payment allocation, overdue account management, billing disputes, credit notes, and receivables reporting.

It applies to commercial transactions involving software services, cloud infrastructure solutions, technical consulting, implementation projects, recurring subscriptions, and other services provided by AR Cloud.

Finance manages the financial aspects of customer billing and accounts receivable. Commercial teams, project managers, and service owners provide the operational information required to verify billing conditions.

This procedure does not establish commercial pricing or authorize contract changes. Those decisions must follow the applicable commercial and management approval processes.

## Customer account creation

Before issuing an invoice, Finance must ensure that the customer has an appropriate billing record in the approved financial system.

Customer records should include the legal or commercial name, billing address, applicable tax identification, authorized billing contact, and relevant contractual references.

The responsible commercial or account management team must provide accurate customer information when establishing a new business relationship.

Finance verifies whether the customer already exists in the system to prevent duplicate account creation.

Changes to sensitive customer information must be reviewed before being applied.

Customer records must not contain unnecessary personal information unrelated to legitimate billing activities.

Access to customer financial records must be restricted to authorized personnel.

## Billing authorization and contractual verification

Customer invoices must be based on valid commercial agreements, approved service orders, or other authorized business arrangements.

Finance verifies that the billing request corresponds to an established contractual obligation or approved charge.

The responsible account manager or service owner must confirm the applicable services, billing period, quantities, and agreed pricing conditions.

Invoices must not be issued for services that were not contracted or otherwise authorized.

Special discounts, pricing adjustments, or non-standard payment terms must be supported by appropriate approval.

Finance may request clarification when the billing request does not match available contractual documentation.

The authorization record must be retained to support future billing reviews and dispute resolution.

## Billing models and service categories

AR Cloud may use different billing models depending on the nature of the services provided.

Recurring subscriptions may be billed monthly, quarterly, or annually according to the customer agreement.

Implementation projects may involve milestone-based billing linked to agreed deliverables.

Professional consulting services may be billed according to fixed fees, approved work periods, or documented service consumption.

Cloud infrastructure services may include usage-based charges when supported by the contractual arrangement.

Finance must identify the applicable billing model before preparing an invoice.

Employees must not apply a billing model that differs from the approved agreement without appropriate authorization.

## Recurring subscription billing

Recurring customer subscriptions must be billed according to the frequency and conditions established in the applicable agreement.

Finance maintains or accesses records identifying active subscriptions, billing cycles, agreed amounts, and relevant customer references.

Service owners must communicate authorized subscription activations, cancellations, upgrades, and downgrades.

Changes affecting recurring charges must be reflected in the appropriate billing period.

Finance must investigate unexpected differences between current charges and previous billing records.

Automatic billing processes must be monitored to detect missing, duplicated, or incorrect invoices.

Recurring charges must not continue after an authorized cancellation takes effect unless a valid contractual obligation remains.

## Project milestone billing

Certain customer projects may be invoiced when agreed milestones are completed.

Project managers are responsible for confirming whether the relevant deliverable or contractual milestone has been achieved.

Finance must receive sufficient documentation before issuing a milestone-based invoice.

Supporting information may include approved project records, customer acceptance confirmations, or other evidence required by the agreement.

A planned milestone date does not automatically establish that the milestone has been completed.

When customer acceptance is contractually required, Finance must verify the applicable acceptance status.

Disputed or incomplete milestones must be escalated before the corresponding invoice is finalized.

## Usage-based service billing

Some AR Cloud services may generate charges based on actual customer consumption.

Usage-based billing must rely on approved measurement sources and the pricing conditions established in the customer agreement.

Technology or the responsible service owner provides the usage records needed to calculate billable amounts.

Finance verifies that the applicable rates, billing periods, and units of measurement are consistent with contractual conditions.

Unusual consumption patterns may require additional validation before invoicing.

Estimated usage must not be presented as confirmed consumption unless the agreement explicitly permits the relevant estimation method.

Corrections to usage data must be documented and reflected in the appropriate billing records.

## Invoice preparation and required information

Customer invoices must include sufficient information to identify the transaction and support accurate payment processing.

Required information generally includes customer identification, invoice number, issue date, billing period, description of services, currency, amounts, applicable taxes, and payment terms.

Invoices must use the authorized pricing and billing conditions associated with the customer agreement.

Finance verifies that invoice calculations are accurate before issuance.

Invoice descriptions should be clear enough for the customer to understand the charges.

Incomplete or inconsistent billing information must be corrected before the invoice is finalized.

Invoices must comply with applicable invoicing and tax requirements.

## Invoice numbering and traceability

Finance maintains a controlled invoice numbering process to support accounting accuracy and document traceability.

Each issued invoice must have a unique identifier within the applicable numbering system.

Invoice references must remain consistent across customer communications, accounting records, and payment allocation activities.

Employees must not manually reuse invoice numbers or create unauthorized replacement invoices.

Cancelled or corrected invoices must be handled through the approved financial process.

Finance must preserve sufficient records to explain gaps, corrections, or other numbering exceptions.

Invoice traceability supports customer inquiries, reconciliation, and financial reviews.

## Invoice issuance and delivery

After validation, Finance issues invoices through the company's approved billing system or authorized financial process.

Invoices must be delivered to the billing contact or channel established for the customer.

Finance should retain evidence of issuance and, when available, delivery confirmation.

An invoice must not be considered paid merely because it has been delivered successfully.

Delivery failures must be investigated and corrected promptly.

When a customer requires submission through a procurement portal or other approved platform, Finance follows the documented submission requirements.

Sensitive billing information must not be distributed to unauthorized recipients.

## Payment terms and customer obligations

Customer payment terms must correspond to the conditions established in the applicable commercial agreement.

Finance records invoice due dates and monitors payment status throughout the collection process.

Standard payment terms must not be changed informally by employees without appropriate authorization.

Customers may have different contractual arrangements depending on service type, agreement duration, or approved commercial conditions.

Finance must distinguish between invoices that are not yet due and those that have become overdue.

Payment term exceptions must be documented and approved by the designated authority.

Accurate due date records are essential for receivables reporting and collection planning.

## Customer payment receipt

Customer payments must be recorded using verified financial information.

Finance reviews bank transactions, payment processor records, and other approved sources to identify incoming payments.

Each payment should be associated with the correct customer and supporting transaction reference.

Payments may cover a single invoice or multiple outstanding invoices.

Finance must investigate unexpected payments or transactions that cannot be associated with a known customer obligation.

Employees must not record an unverified payment as completed solely because a customer provides a screenshot or informal confirmation.

Confirmed payments must be reflected in the relevant accounting and customer balance records.

## Payment allocation and reconciliation

Finance allocates received payments to the corresponding customer invoices.

Allocation should consider payment references, amounts, currencies, customer instructions, and outstanding balances.

When a payment covers multiple invoices, Finance must document the distribution of the received amount.

Partial payments must reduce the outstanding balance without incorrectly marking the invoice as fully settled.

Overpayments must be investigated and handled according to approved financial procedures.

Unapplied payments must remain identifiable until the appropriate allocation is confirmed.

Payment allocation records must support subsequent customer balance reconciliation.

## Partial payments and outstanding balances

Customers may occasionally submit payments that do not cover the full amount of an invoice.

Finance records the amount received and maintains the remaining unpaid balance.

Partial payment does not automatically cancel the customer's obligation to settle the outstanding amount.

The responsible account manager may be consulted when a partial payment results from a commercial disagreement or negotiated arrangement.

Any approved installment agreement must be documented.

Employees must not independently forgive unpaid balances without the required financial authorization.

Finance continues monitoring outstanding amounts until they are paid, adjusted, or resolved through an authorized process.

## Accounts receivable aging

Finance prepares accounts receivable aging information to monitor unpaid customer balances.

Aging reports classify outstanding invoices according to how long they have remained unpaid.

Categories may include invoices not yet due and invoices overdue by 1–30, 31–60, 61–90, or more than 90 days.

The aging report helps Finance identify collection priorities and potential payment risks.

Balances must reflect valid invoices, received payments, credit notes, and authorized adjustments.

Unusual or significantly overdue balances may require management review.

Aging information must be updated regularly to support accurate financial decisions.

## Overdue payment reminders

Finance coordinates payment reminders for invoices that remain unpaid after their due dates.

Initial reminders should identify the relevant invoice, outstanding amount, original due date, and approved payment instructions.

Communications must remain professional, accurate, and consistent with customer agreements.

Finance must verify that the payment has not already been received before issuing an overdue notice.

Customers who report a payment in progress may be asked to provide an appropriate transaction reference.

Reminder activity should be documented to maintain a clear collection history.

Employees must not threaten unauthorized penalties or service restrictions during routine collection communications.

## Collection escalation process

Invoices that remain overdue after routine reminders may require additional escalation.

Finance reviews the outstanding amount, duration of delay, customer payment history, and any known disputes.

The responsible account manager may be involved when collection concerns could affect an ongoing customer relationship.

Significant overdue balances may require review by authorized management personnel.

Any decision to suspend services, change commercial conditions, or pursue formal recovery measures must follow applicable contractual and organizational requirements.

Finance must not independently authorize service suspension when that responsibility belongs to another designated authority.

Escalation decisions must be documented and communicated to relevant stakeholders.

## Customer billing disputes

Customers may dispute invoices because of pricing differences, incorrect quantities, service delivery concerns, or other billing issues.

Finance records the dispute and identifies the affected invoice and amount.

The responsible account manager, project manager, or service owner provides information needed to verify the disputed charges.

Finance compares the invoice with applicable agreements, service records, and authorized pricing conditions.

The undisputed portion of an invoice may remain payable according to contractual terms.

Disputed amounts must not be altered or removed without appropriate documentation and authorization.

Finance communicates the resolution through approved customer channels.

## Credit notes and billing corrections

Credit notes may be required when a customer invoice contains an error or an authorized commercial adjustment.

Examples include incorrect pricing, duplicate billing, service cancellation adjustments, or other validated discrepancies.

Finance verifies the reason for the credit note and obtains any required approval.

Credit notes must reference the affected invoice or transaction when applicable.

Employees must not issue credit notes to conceal unauthorized discounts or accounting errors.

Corrections must be reflected accurately in customer balances and financial records.

When an invoice has already been paid, Finance determines whether the correction requires a refund or credit toward future charges.

## Customer refunds

Customer refunds may arise from overpayments, duplicate payments, authorized cancellations, or other valid financial adjustments.

Refund requests must be reviewed by Finance and supported by appropriate transaction records.

Finance verifies the customer identity, original payment, refundable amount, and authorized destination for the funds.

Refunds must not be directed to unrelated third-party accounts without an approved and documented justification.

Requests involving changed banking instructions require additional verification.

Refund approval and execution must follow the organization's financial authorization requirements.

Completed refunds must be recorded and reconciled with the corresponding customer balance.

## Foreign currency customer billing

AR Cloud may issue invoices in foreign currencies when permitted by the applicable customer agreement.

Finance records the original invoice currency and amount.

Foreign currency transactions must follow the organization's accounting and conversion procedures.

Differences between invoice recognition and payment settlement may result in exchange rate adjustments.

Finance must distinguish exchange rate differences from unpaid customer balances.

Employees must not independently change the invoice currency or conversion method to alter the amount due.

Relevant banking fees and payment deductions must be reviewed and documented when necessary.

## Customer credit risk monitoring

Finance may monitor customer payment behavior to identify potential collection risks.

Relevant indicators include repeated late payments, increasing outstanding balances, disputed invoices, and broken payment commitments.

Finance may communicate significant concerns to authorized commercial or management personnel.

Credit risk assessments must rely on relevant business information and must not involve inappropriate use of confidential customer data.

Customers with material overdue balances may require additional review before new commercial commitments are approved.

Finance must not independently modify contractual credit terms without appropriate authority.

Monitoring results may inform collection priorities and financial forecasting.

## Bad debt assessment and write-offs

Some customer balances may become difficult or unlikely to collect.

Finance periodically evaluates outstanding receivables for potential impairment or expected credit losses according to applicable accounting policies.

The assessment may consider the age of the balance, available collection information, disputes, and relevant customer circumstances.

An overdue invoice must not automatically be written off solely because a payment deadline has passed.

Write-offs require appropriate financial authorization and supporting documentation.

Writing off an accounting balance does not necessarily eliminate the customer's legal obligation.

Finance retains records explaining significant write-off decisions and related accounting adjustments.

## Accounts receivable reporting

Finance prepares accounts receivable reports to support financial management and cash flow planning.

Reports may include total outstanding balances, overdue amounts, aging categories, payment trends, disputed invoices, and significant customer exposures.

Information must be reconciled with the relevant accounting records.

Reports should distinguish confirmed payments from expected collections.

Finance may identify customers or invoices requiring additional management attention.

Sensitive customer financial information must be shared only with authorized personnel.

Receivables reports support decision-making but must not present uncertain future payments as guaranteed income.

## Month-end accounts receivable closing

Finance reviews customer billing and payment records as part of the monthly accounting close.

The review includes verifying issued invoices, recorded payments, credit notes, outstanding balances, and unresolved allocation differences.

Invoices and related financial activity must be recorded in the appropriate accounting period.

Finance may coordinate with service owners to identify billable activities that have not yet been invoiced.

Unresolved material discrepancies must be investigated before finalizing the relevant accounting balances.

Accounting adjustments must follow approved procedures and retain sufficient supporting evidence.

Closing records must support reconciliation with financial statements and management reports.

## Customer communication and confidentiality

Customer billing communications must be conducted through approved company channels.

Finance must verify the recipient before disclosing invoices, account statements, or payment information.

Employees must not share customer financial records with unauthorized third parties.

Sensitive banking information must be handled according to applicable security requirements.

Customers must receive clear and accurate information about their invoices and outstanding balances.

When a billing issue requires input from another department, only the information necessary to resolve the issue should be shared.

Suspected phishing, payment redirection attempts, or fraudulent billing communications must be reported promptly.

## Billing system controls

Access to billing and accounts receivable systems must be restricted according to assigned responsibilities.

Authorized personnel may create invoices, register payments, issue credit notes, or review customer balances according to their permissions.

Where practical, sensitive adjustments should require independent review.

System activity should preserve appropriate transaction history and auditability.

Employees must not share credentials or bypass approval controls.

Finance coordinates with Technology when billing systems require access changes, technical support, or security investigation.

Unexpected changes to billing data must be reviewed and corrected through authorized procedures.

## Roles and responsibilities

Finance administers customer invoicing, payment registration, receivables monitoring, reconciliations, and financial reporting.

Commercial and account management teams provide approved contractual information and coordinate customer relationship matters.

Project managers confirm relevant milestones and deliverables when billing depends on project progress.

Service owners provide operational records needed for recurring or usage-based billing.

Technology supports the security and availability of approved billing systems and related technical integrations.

Authorized management personnel review significant collection risks, exceptional adjustments, and matters requiring additional approval.

All participants must maintain accurate records and protect confidential customer information.

## Procedure review and continuous improvement

Finance periodically reviews billing and collection activities to identify recurring errors, payment delays, disputed charges, and control weaknesses.

Reviews may consider invoice accuracy, overdue balances, payment allocation issues, customer feedback, and collection effectiveness.

Corrective actions may include improved billing validation, revised communication procedures, additional training, or system enhancements.

Changes to billing responsibilities and approval controls must be communicated to affected personnel.

Exceptions must be documented and approved by the appropriate financial authority.

This procedure complements the Financial Reporting and Internal Controls Procedure, Budget Planning and Monitoring Policy, and applicable customer contract management requirements.
