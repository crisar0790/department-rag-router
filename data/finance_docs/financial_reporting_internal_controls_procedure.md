
# Financial Reporting and Internal Controls Procedure

Document ID: FIN-005
Department: Finance
Version: 1.0
Effective date: 2026-01-01
Company: AR Cloud
Document type: Synthetic procedure for an educational project

## Purpose and scope

AR Cloud maintains financial reporting and internal control procedures to ensure that financial information is accurate, complete, consistent, and available to authorized decision-makers.

This procedure defines how Finance prepares financial reports, validates accounting information, performs reconciliations, investigates discrepancies, and maintains controls over financial records.

It applies to financial transactions and accounting information generated by company operations, including revenue, expenses, assets, liabilities, and other relevant financial activities.

The procedure supports management decision-making, financial accountability, and the preparation of reliable financial statements.

Finance coordinates financial reporting activities and maintains the official accounting records.

Other departments must provide accurate supporting information when their activities affect financial reporting.

## Financial reporting objectives

Financial reports must provide a reliable representation of AR Cloud's financial position and operating performance.

Reports should be accurate, complete, understandable, and prepared within the required reporting periods.

Finance must apply consistent accounting classifications and documented reporting methods.

Financial information must not be intentionally modified to produce misleading results.

Reports should distinguish actual recorded transactions from estimates, projections, and assumptions.

Significant uncertainties or limitations must be disclosed to authorized report recipients when relevant.

Financial reporting must support management decisions without concealing material financial risks.

## Accounting records and transaction registration

Finance maintains accounting records for financial transactions conducted by AR Cloud.

Transactions must be supported by appropriate documentation and recorded using approved accounting procedures.

Each accounting entry should identify the transaction date, amount, relevant accounts, business purpose, and supporting reference.

Transactions must be classified according to the applicable chart of accounts.

Employees must not create fictitious transactions or modify records to conceal unauthorized activity.

Accounting corrections must preserve sufficient information to explain the original entry and the reason for adjustment.

Finance must ensure that financial records remain traceable to their supporting documents.

## Chart of accounts management

AR Cloud uses a structured chart of accounts to classify financial activity consistently.

The chart of accounts organizes transactions into categories such as assets, liabilities, equity, revenue, and expenses.

Finance is responsible for maintaining account definitions and ensuring that transactions are assigned appropriately.

Requests to create new accounts must include a legitimate accounting or reporting justification.

Unnecessary duplication of accounts should be avoided because it can reduce reporting consistency.

Changes to account classifications must be reviewed for their effect on historical comparisons and financial statements.

Employees outside authorized Finance roles must not independently modify the official chart of accounts.

## Monthly financial closing process

Finance performs a monthly closing process to verify accounting completeness and prepare financial information for management review.

The closing process includes reviewing recorded transactions, identifying missing entries, validating account balances, and completing required reconciliations.

Finance establishes internal deadlines for receiving supporting documentation from other departments.

Transactions must be recorded in the appropriate accounting period according to applicable accounting policies.

Unresolved discrepancies must be investigated before the affected accounts are considered finalized.

Material adjustments must be documented and reviewed by an authorized financial representative.

Once the closing process is completed, Finance prepares the required financial reports.

## Revenue recognition and validation

Finance reviews revenue-related records to ensure that reported income corresponds to legitimate business activities.

Revenue must be recorded according to applicable accounting policies and the nature of the underlying transaction.

Supporting documentation may include customer contracts, issued invoices, service delivery records, and payment confirmations.

Finance must distinguish between invoiced amounts, recognized revenue, and cash received.

Customer payments do not automatically establish that all related revenue recognition conditions have been satisfied.

Unexpected differences between operational records and accounting balances must be investigated.

Revenue must not be intentionally recorded in an incorrect period to manipulate reported performance.

## Expense recognition and classification

Finance records expenses according to their economic nature and the accounting period to which they relate.

Expenses may arise from supplier invoices, payroll-related obligations, subscriptions, professional services, or other authorized activities.

The accounting treatment must be consistent with applicable policies and supported by relevant documentation.

Finance must distinguish between expenses already paid and obligations that remain outstanding.

Certain expenditures may require asset recognition or allocation across multiple reporting periods rather than immediate expense recognition.

Department managers may be consulted when the purpose or classification of an expense is unclear.

Incorrect classifications must be corrected through documented accounting adjustments.

## Bank account reconciliation

Finance periodically reconciles bank account activity with the company's accounting records.

Reconciliation compares bank statement transactions with recorded deposits, withdrawals, transfers, fees, and other financial movements.

Unmatched transactions must be investigated to determine whether they represent timing differences, missing entries, errors, or potentially unauthorized activity.

Finance must document the reason for material reconciliation differences.

Bank charges and interest must be recorded using appropriate accounting classifications.

Reconciliation should be reviewed by an authorized person who did not perform the underlying payment activity whenever practical.

Bank reconciliation records must be retained as supporting evidence for financial reporting.

## Accounts receivable reconciliation

Finance reviews customer-related balances to verify the accuracy of outstanding receivables.

The reconciliation process compares customer invoices, recorded payments, credit notes, and accounting balances.

Unapplied payments must be investigated and allocated to the appropriate customer records.

Disputed invoices and overdue balances must be identified for appropriate follow-up.

Finance must not remove outstanding receivables from accounting records without a documented and authorized adjustment.

Differences between customer statements and internal records must be investigated.

Significant receivable issues may require management review when they affect expected cash collections or reported financial results.

## Accounts payable reconciliation

Finance reconciles supplier balances with recorded invoices, payments, credit notes, and outstanding obligations.

The process verifies that valid supplier liabilities are accurately reflected in the accounting records.

Unrecorded invoices, duplicate entries, and incorrect payment allocations must be investigated.

Supplier statements may be used as supporting evidence when available.

Accounts payable reconciliation must remain consistent with the Invoice Processing and Accounts Payable Procedure.

Material discrepancies must be documented and resolved through authorized corrections.

Reconciliation results support accurate liability reporting and financial closing activities.

## Fixed asset accounting controls

AR Cloud may acquire equipment and other assets that require ongoing accounting management.

Finance maintains records of qualifying fixed assets according to applicable accounting policies.

Asset records should identify acquisition cost, acquisition date, assigned classification, and relevant supporting documentation.

Technology or other responsible departments may provide information about asset assignment, condition, or disposal.

Depreciation and other accounting adjustments must follow established financial rules.

Asset disposals, losses, or transfers may require accounting review and authorization.

Finance periodically evaluates whether asset records remain consistent with available operational information.

## Accruals and accounting estimates

Some financial obligations or expenses must be recognized before a corresponding invoice or payment is received.

Finance evaluates whether accruals are necessary to reflect transactions in the appropriate accounting period.

Accrual calculations must be supported by reasonable assumptions and available information.

Examples may include services received but not yet invoiced or other obligations attributable to the reporting period.

Estimates must not be intentionally exaggerated or understated to influence financial results.

Significant assumptions must be documented and reviewed when necessary.

Accruals and estimates should be reassessed when more accurate information becomes available.

## Journal entries and accounting adjustments

Journal entries are used to record financial transactions and authorized accounting adjustments.

Entries must contain a clear description, appropriate account classifications, and supporting documentation.

Manual adjustments require particular attention because they may affect reported financial results without originating from an automated operational process.

Finance must establish authorization requirements for material or unusual journal entries.

Employees must not create adjustments to conceal missing documentation or unauthorized transactions.

Corrections must be traceable and must not destroy the original accounting history.

Unusual or high-impact adjustments may require additional review before posting.

## Segregation of financial duties

AR Cloud applies segregation of duties to reduce the risk of errors, unauthorized transactions, and financial misconduct.

Where practical, responsibility for initiating, approving, executing, and reconciling financial transactions should be distributed among different authorized individuals.

An employee who prepares a payment should not independently perform every stage of its approval and reconciliation.

When organizational size or operational circumstances limit full segregation, Finance must establish compensating controls.

Compensating controls may include independent management review, transaction monitoring, or periodic verification.

Access permissions must reflect assigned responsibilities.

Segregation requirements must be reviewed when employees change roles or leave the organization.

## Financial system access controls

Access to accounting and financial reporting systems must be restricted to authorized personnel.

Permissions should be assigned according to legitimate business responsibilities.

Employees must not share accounts, passwords, or authentication credentials.

Access to sensitive financial functions may require additional authentication or approval controls.

Finance coordinates with Technology to establish and maintain appropriate system access.

Permissions must be reviewed periodically and updated when responsibilities change.

Unauthorized access attempts or suspicious activity must be reported through the appropriate security channels.

## Financial data integrity and change tracking

Financial systems must preserve the integrity and traceability of accounting information.

Changes to financial records should be attributable to authorized users and supported by an appropriate business reason.

Audit trails should capture relevant information about material record modifications when supported by the system.

Employees must not disable logging or bypass approved controls to alter financial data.

Finance must investigate unexplained changes to accounting balances or transaction details.

Technology may assist with technical investigation when data integrity concerns involve system behavior or unauthorized access.

Material integrity incidents must be escalated according to organizational procedures.

## Financial statement preparation

Finance prepares financial statements using validated accounting records and applicable reporting requirements.

Depending on organizational needs, statements may include a balance sheet, income statement, cash flow statement, and supporting schedules.

Reported figures must reconcile with the relevant accounting records.

Finance must review significant balances and unusual changes before distributing financial statements.

Material accounting estimates and relevant limitations must be considered during preparation.

Draft financial statements must be clearly distinguished from approved final versions.

Only authorized personnel may approve or distribute official financial statements.

## Management reporting

Finance provides financial reports to authorized managers to support operational and strategic decisions.

Management reports may include revenue performance, operating expenses, profitability indicators, cash flow information, and significant financial risks.

Reports must use consistent definitions and clearly identify the reporting period.

When comparisons are provided, Finance should explain material differences that could affect interpretation.

Financial indicators must not be presented without sufficient context when doing so could lead to misleading conclusions.

Sensitive information must be shared only with authorized recipients.

Management may request additional analysis when financial results differ materially from expectations.

## Internal control monitoring

Finance periodically evaluates whether financial controls operate as intended.

Monitoring may include reviewing approvals, examining accounting adjustments, verifying reconciliations, and identifying unusual transaction patterns.

Control weaknesses must be documented and assessed according to their potential financial impact.

Finance may recommend changes to procedures, system permissions, or review responsibilities.

Recurring control failures may require management attention and corrective action.

Employees must cooperate with authorized internal reviews and provide relevant supporting information.

Control monitoring should support continuous improvement rather than relying exclusively on the detection of errors after they occur.

## Financial discrepancies and error correction

Employees who identify financial discrepancies must report them promptly to Finance.

Examples include incorrect account balances, duplicate transactions, missing entries, unexplained payments, and inconsistent supporting documentation.

Finance evaluates the nature, cause, and potential impact of the discrepancy.

Corrections must be supported by appropriate documentation and authorized according to their significance.

Material errors affecting previously issued reports may require management review and additional corrective procedures.

Employees must not conceal known discrepancies or create unsupported adjustments to eliminate them.

The resolution of significant errors must be documented for future reference.

## Suspected fraud and financial irregularities

Financial irregularities may involve unauthorized payments, falsified documentation, intentional misclassification, or misuse of company resources.

Employees must report suspected irregularities through appropriate internal channels.

Reports must be handled with suitable confidentiality and without unnecessary disclosure.

Finance must preserve relevant financial records when an investigation is required.

Employees must not independently alter suspected fraudulent records or interfere with evidence.

Management may involve authorized specialists or other departments depending on the nature of the incident.

Confirmed issues may result in corrective controls, financial recovery actions, or other measures consistent with applicable requirements.

## Financial document retention

Finance maintains accounting records and supporting documentation according to applicable retention requirements.

Relevant records may include journal entries, invoices, payment confirmations, bank statements, reconciliations, financial statements, and approval records.

Documents must be stored in approved systems with appropriate access restrictions.

Retention practices must preserve the availability and integrity of records needed for reporting and review.

Employees must not delete financial documentation solely because a transaction has been completed.

Disposal of records must follow authorized retention and deletion procedures.

Confidential financial information must remain protected throughout its lifecycle.

## Reporting deadlines and escalation

Finance establishes reporting schedules for monthly, quarterly, and annual financial activities.

Departments must provide required information within the communicated deadlines.

Delays involving significant financial records must be reported to the responsible Finance representative.

Finance evaluates whether missing information could materially affect reporting accuracy.

When a deadline cannot be met, the responsible personnel must communicate the cause and expected resolution.

Management must be informed when material reporting risks remain unresolved.

Reporting deadlines must not be used as justification for knowingly issuing inaccurate financial information.

## Roles and responsibilities

Finance maintains accounting records, performs reconciliations, prepares financial reports, and coordinates internal control activities.

Department managers provide accurate operational information and review relevant financial matters within their responsibilities.

Technology supports the availability, security, and controlled access of financial information systems.

Authorized management personnel review and approve financial reports according to assigned authority.

Employees must submit accurate financial documentation and promptly report known errors or irregularities.

Personnel responsible for financial controls must document relevant review activities and corrective actions.

All participants must preserve confidentiality and comply with applicable financial procedures.

## Procedure review and continuous improvement

Finance periodically reviews financial reporting and internal control procedures to identify opportunities for improving reliability and efficiency.

Reviews may consider reconciliation findings, accounting errors, reporting delays, access control issues, and recurring documentation problems.

Corrective actions may include improved review procedures, revised authorization responsibilities, additional training, or financial system improvements.

Significant changes must be communicated to affected departments.

Exceptions must be documented and approved by the appropriate financial authority.

This procedure complements the Budget Planning and Monitoring Policy, Invoice Processing and Accounts Payable Procedure, and other applicable financial and information security requirements.
