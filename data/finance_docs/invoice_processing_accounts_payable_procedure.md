
# Invoice Processing and Accounts Payable Procedure

Document ID: FIN-003
Department: Finance
Version: 1.0
Effective date: 2026-01-01
Company: AR Cloud
Document type: Synthetic procedure for an educational project

## Purpose and scope

AR Cloud maintains an accounts payable process to ensure that supplier invoices are recorded accurately, verified against authorized business commitments, and paid according to approved financial conditions.

This procedure establishes the responsibilities and controls associated with receiving invoices, validating supporting documentation, recording liabilities, managing payment schedules, resolving discrepancies, and maintaining financial records.

It applies to invoices issued by suppliers of goods, software, cloud infrastructure, professional services, subscriptions, and other authorized business expenses.

Finance is responsible for administering accounts payable activities. Department managers and service owners provide operational confirmation when invoices relate to purchases or services under their responsibility.

This procedure complements the Purchase Requests and Approval Procedure. Purchasing authorization establishes whether AR Cloud may acquire goods or services, while accounts payable controls determine whether a resulting supplier invoice is valid and ready for payment.

## Invoice receipt and registration

Supplier invoices must be submitted through the company's approved financial communication or document management channels.

Employees who receive invoices outside the established process must forward them to Finance without unnecessary delay.

Finance records each invoice using the information required for financial tracking and accounting.

The registration process should capture the supplier name, invoice number, issue date, due date, currency, total amount, applicable taxes, and associated purchase reference when available.

Invoices must be assigned an internal tracking reference to support subsequent review and payment reconciliation.

Finance must avoid registering the same invoice multiple times.

An invoice received by an employee does not automatically establish that the corresponding purchase was authorized or that payment is due.

## Required invoice information

Invoices must contain sufficient information to identify the supplier, the transaction, and the financial obligation.

Required information generally includes the supplier's legal or commercial identification, invoice number, issue date, description of goods or services, applicable amounts, currency, and payment conditions.

Tax information must be reviewed according to the requirements applicable to the transaction.

Invoices related to purchase orders should reference the corresponding order when the supplier can provide that information.

Finance may request corrections when invoice descriptions are incomplete or inconsistent with the approved purchase.

Documents that do not satisfy applicable invoicing requirements must not be processed as valid supplier invoices without appropriate review.

The absence of required information must be documented and resolved before final payment authorization.

## Supplier verification

Finance verifies that the invoice was issued by a legitimate supplier associated with an authorized business transaction.

Supplier information should be compared with approved vendor records and relevant purchasing documentation.

New suppliers may require additional verification before their invoices can be processed.

Finance must investigate unexpected changes in supplier identity, payment details, or contractual conditions.

An invoice from a previously used supplier is not automatically valid if the underlying purchase cannot be confirmed.

Employees must not create or modify supplier records without the required authorization.

Supplier verification helps reduce the risk of fraudulent invoices, duplicate payments, and unauthorized financial commitments.

## Invoice matching and purchase references

Invoices associated with approved purchase orders must be checked against the corresponding purchasing records.

Finance compares the invoiced products or services, quantities, prices, and relevant contractual conditions with the authorized commitment.

When applicable, the verification process also includes confirmation that the goods or services were received.

This process may involve matching the purchase order, supplier invoice, and delivery or acceptance record.

Differences between these records must be investigated before payment approval.

Minor administrative discrepancies may be resolved through documented clarification, while material differences may require additional authorization.

Finance must not modify purchase records solely to make an incorrect invoice appear consistent with the approved order.

## Service acceptance and delivery confirmation

Invoices for professional services, software subscriptions, cloud services, and other non-physical purchases may require confirmation from the responsible service owner.

The service owner verifies whether the supplier delivered the agreed service or completed the relevant contractual milestone.

For recurring services, acceptance may involve confirming that the subscription or service remained available during the invoiced period.

For project-based services, the responsible manager may need to verify agreed deliverables before authorizing payment.

Disputed or incomplete services must be reported to Finance.

Finance may place the affected invoice on hold while the discrepancy is investigated.

Payment approval must not be based solely on the supplier's assertion that a service was delivered.

## Duplicate invoice prevention

Finance applies controls to identify invoices that may have already been registered or paid.

Duplicate checks should consider supplier identity, invoice number, amount, currency, and transaction dates.

Invoices with different document numbers may still represent duplicate charges when they relate to the same underlying transaction.

Recurring subscriptions require particular attention because similar amounts may appear in successive billing periods.

When a potential duplicate is identified, Finance must investigate the related records before authorizing payment.

Confirmed duplicates must be documented and excluded from further payment processing.

If a duplicate payment has already occurred, Finance coordinates recovery or correction with the supplier.

## Invoice approval workflow

An invoice must complete the applicable validation and approval steps before payment processing.

Finance verifies financial documentation and confirms that the invoice corresponds to an authorized business obligation.

The responsible manager or service owner may be required to confirm delivery, service acceptance, or the legitimacy of the charge.

Approval responsibilities must remain consistent with the organization's financial authorization procedures.

An employee must not approve an invoice when doing so creates an unmanaged conflict of interest.

Invoices requiring additional clarification must remain pending until the necessary information is available.

The final approval decision must be recorded in the approved financial system or supporting documentation.

## Payment terms and due dates

Finance monitors invoice due dates to support timely payment and avoid unnecessary penalties.

Payment conditions may depend on supplier agreements, purchase orders, contractual milestones, or other authorized arrangements.

Invoices must be scheduled according to their approved terms and the company's financial processing requirements.

An invoice due date does not override the requirement to complete appropriate verification.

Finance may prioritize invoices approaching their due dates when the required approvals and supporting documentation are complete.

Requests for early payment must include an appropriate business justification.

Changes to payment terms must be documented and authorized by the responsible financial personnel.

## Payment scheduling and cash flow coordination

Accounts payable activities must be coordinated with AR Cloud's cash flow planning.

Finance reviews upcoming obligations to identify expected payment requirements and potential concentration of expenses.

Payment schedules should consider invoice due dates, available funds, operational priorities, and authorized contractual commitments.

Finance may coordinate with management when significant payments affect short-term liquidity.

Cash flow limitations do not authorize employees to conceal obligations or alter accounting records.

When payment delays are expected, Finance determines the appropriate communication and escalation process.

Payment planning must preserve accurate records of outstanding liabilities regardless of the expected settlement date.

## Supplier banking information and fraud prevention

Supplier banking information must be handled through approved financial systems and verification procedures.

Requests to change bank accounts or payment destinations require independent verification using a trusted communication channel.

Finance must not rely solely on an email requesting urgent payment to a new account.

Unexpected changes in payment instructions may indicate attempted invoice fraud or supplier impersonation.

Employees must not disclose confidential supplier information to unauthorized individuals.

Banking credentials and authentication codes must never be requested or shared through informal communication channels.

Suspicious payment requests must be reported to the responsible financial authority and, when relevant, the Technology Department.

## Payment execution and authorization

Payments may be executed only after the invoice has completed the required validation and authorization process.

Finance prepares payment instructions using verified supplier information and approved payment details.

Where applicable, payment preparation and final authorization should be performed by different authorized individuals.

The payment record must identify the supplier, amount, currency, invoice reference, and execution date.

Employees must not initiate payments outside their assigned financial authority.

Payment confirmations must be retained to support reconciliation and future inquiries.

A payment should not be marked as completed until its execution has been confirmed through an appropriate financial record.

## Foreign currency invoices

AR Cloud may receive supplier invoices denominated in currencies other than the company's primary accounting currency.

Finance records the original invoice amount and currency.

Currency conversion must follow the organization's established accounting policies and applicable financial requirements.

Exchange rate differences between invoice registration and payment execution may require separate accounting treatment.

Employees must not select arbitrary exchange rates to change the reported value of an obligation.

Banking fees and foreign transaction charges must be documented when relevant.

Finance may consult external financial or accounting specialists when a transaction involves complex international payment requirements.

## Credit notes and invoice adjustments

Suppliers may issue credit notes to correct overcharges, returned goods, cancelled services, or other billing discrepancies.

Finance must verify that the credit note corresponds to a legitimate adjustment.

The credit note should reference the original invoice or transaction when applicable.

Adjustments must be recorded accurately to prevent incorrect outstanding balances.

Employees must not use credit notes to conceal unauthorized purchases or unrelated financial transactions.

If an invoice has already been paid, Finance determines whether the adjustment requires a refund or credit against future supplier charges.

All significant adjustments must be supported by appropriate documentation.

## Disputed invoices and payment holds

Finance may place an invoice on hold when the amount, supplier identity, delivery confirmation, or authorization status is disputed.

The reason for the hold must be documented.

The responsible department or service owner must provide information necessary to resolve operational discrepancies.

Finance coordinates communication with the supplier when billing corrections or additional documentation are required.

An invoice must not be released for payment until material discrepancies have been resolved or an authorized exception has been approved.

Payment holds must be reviewed periodically to prevent unresolved invoices from remaining unattended.

Disputes that could affect contractual obligations or critical services must be escalated to the appropriate stakeholders.

## Accounts payable reconciliation

Finance periodically reconciles accounts payable records with supplier statements, payment confirmations, and accounting balances.

Reconciliation helps identify unpaid invoices, duplicate records, missing payments, incorrect allocations, and other discrepancies.

Supplier balances must reflect valid outstanding obligations after recorded payments and adjustments.

Differences must be investigated and corrected using appropriate accounting entries.

Employees must not delete historical records merely to eliminate an unexplained discrepancy.

Significant reconciliation issues may require management review.

Reconciliation results should be documented to support financial accuracy and internal accountability.

## Month-end and period-end closing

Accounts payable records contribute to AR Cloud's financial reporting and accounting close activities.

Finance reviews outstanding invoices and relevant obligations before closing a reporting period.

Expenses must be recorded in the appropriate accounting period according to applicable accounting policies.

Goods or services received before period-end may require accounting recognition even when the related invoice has not yet arrived.

Finance coordinates with department managers to identify significant unbilled obligations.

Corrections discovered after closing must follow established accounting adjustment procedures.

Employees must not intentionally delay invoice registration to manipulate reported expenses or financial results.

## Supplier inquiries and payment status

Suppliers may contact AR Cloud to request information about invoice receipt, approval, or payment status.

Finance is responsible for responding through approved communication channels.

Before disclosing financial details, personnel must verify that the requester is authorized to receive the information.

Payment status updates should distinguish between received, under review, approved, scheduled, and completed invoices.

Employees must not promise a payment date that has not been confirmed through the approved process.

Disputes involving supplier balances or payment confirmations must be investigated using available financial records.

Sensitive supplier information must remain protected during these communications.

## Record retention and auditability

Finance maintains records of invoices, approvals, purchase references, payment confirmations, credit notes, and relevant supplier communications.

Records must be stored in approved systems with access controls appropriate to their sensitivity.

Financial documentation must be sufficiently complete to support accounting reviews, reconciliation, and internal audits.

Employees must not alter or remove records to conceal unauthorized payments or processing errors.

Retention periods must comply with applicable organizational and legal requirements.

Access to payment records must be limited to authorized personnel with legitimate responsibilities.

Finance periodically reviews documentation quality to identify recurring weaknesses in the accounts payable process.

## Roles and responsibilities

Finance administers invoice registration, financial verification, payment scheduling, reconciliation, and record maintenance.

Department managers confirm business necessity and provide approval or clarification when required.

Service owners validate delivery and performance for purchases under their responsibility.

Employees must promptly forward supplier invoices received outside the approved process.

Technology supports the security and availability of systems used for financial processing and may assist with suspected payment-related cybersecurity incidents.

Authorized financial personnel execute payments according to assigned responsibilities.

All participants must report suspected fraud, duplicate payments, or material accounting discrepancies.

## Procedure review and continuous improvement

Finance periodically reviews accounts payable activities to improve processing accuracy, financial controls, and operational efficiency.

Reviews may consider invoice processing delays, supplier disputes, duplicate payment incidents, reconciliation discrepancies, and documentation problems.

Corrective actions may include revised approval workflows, improved validation controls, additional training, or system configuration changes.

Significant procedural changes must be communicated to affected employees and managers.

Exceptions must be documented and approved by the appropriate financial authority.

This procedure complements the Purchase Requests and Approval Procedure, Employee Expense Reimbursement Policy, and applicable information security requirements.
