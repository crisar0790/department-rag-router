
# Purchase Requests and Approval Procedure

Document ID: FIN-002
Department: Finance
Version: 1.0
Effective date: 2026-01-01
Company: AR Cloud
Document type: Synthetic procedure for an educational project

## Purpose and scope

AR Cloud uses a structured purchasing process to ensure that company funds are spent responsibly, purchases support legitimate business needs, and financial commitments receive appropriate authorization.

This procedure defines how employees request purchases, how managers evaluate business requirements, and how Finance verifies budget availability and approval conditions before an order is placed.

The procedure applies to purchases of equipment, software, subscriptions, professional services, office supplies, external consulting, and other goods or services acquired for company operations.

It covers both one-time purchases and recurring financial commitments. Employee purchases made personally and later submitted for reimbursement are governed by the Employee Expense Reimbursement Policy.

Employees must not commit AR Cloud to a purchase before the required approvals have been obtained.

## Purchase request initiation

Employees who identify a business need must submit a purchase request through the approved internal process.

The request must explain what is needed, why the purchase is necessary, and how it supports an authorized business activity.

Employees should identify the requested product or service, estimated cost, preferred supplier when applicable, expected delivery date, and relevant department or project.

Requests involving recurring services must specify the expected billing frequency and anticipated commitment period.

When a purchase replaces an existing product or service, the requester should explain why the current solution is no longer sufficient.

Incomplete requests may be returned for clarification before further evaluation.

## Business justification requirements

Every purchase request must include a clear business justification.

The justification should explain the operational problem being addressed, the expected benefit, and the consequences of postponing or rejecting the purchase.

Requests for new tools or services should describe whether an existing company resource could satisfy the same requirement.

Managers must evaluate whether the proposed purchase is necessary, proportionate, and aligned with departmental objectives.

A request should not be approved solely because the product is popular, discounted, or preferred by an employee.

For significant expenditures, Finance may request a comparison of alternative solutions or an explanation of expected long-term value.

The business justification must be retained with the purchase record.

## Purchase categories

Finance classifies purchases to support appropriate financial controls and reporting.

Common categories include technology equipment, software licenses, professional services, training, office supplies, operational infrastructure, and general administrative expenses.

Purchases involving technology products or services may require technical review before financial approval.

Requests involving external providers that will access company information may require information security assessment.

Professional services may require additional documentation describing the scope of work, deliverables, and expected duration.

Finance may request category corrections when a purchase has been submitted under an inappropriate classification.

Classification must reflect the actual purpose of the purchase rather than the approval route preferred by the requester.

## Budget availability verification

Finance verifies whether sufficient budget is available before approving a financial commitment.

The review considers the department's approved budget, existing commitments, planned expenditures, and the expected cost of the proposed purchase.

Budget availability does not automatically establish that a purchase is necessary or authorized.

If sufficient budget is unavailable, the request may require additional justification, postponement, budget adjustment, or rejection.

Managers must not divide purchases into smaller requests to conceal their total cost or bypass financial controls.

Finance records relevant budget decisions to support expenditure tracking and future planning.

Significant budget changes must follow the organization's established financial authorization procedures.

## Approval levels and authorization

Purchase approvals depend on the financial amount, business purpose, and operational risk of the request.

Department managers evaluate business necessity and confirm that the requested goods or services support legitimate activities.

Finance verifies budget availability, financial conditions, and compliance with purchasing procedures.

Higher-value or unusual purchases may require authorization from additional designated decision-makers.

Approval authority must be assigned according to documented internal responsibilities.

Employees must not approve purchases that create an unmanaged conflict of interest.

When the requester is also an authorized manager, an independent review may be required according to applicable approval controls.

Approval decisions must be documented before the company makes a binding financial commitment.

## Supplier selection and comparison

AR Cloud seeks suppliers that provide suitable products or services at reasonable cost and acceptable operational risk.

Supplier selection may consider price, quality, reliability, support availability, delivery conditions, contractual terms, and security requirements.

For significant purchases, Finance may request multiple quotations or documented comparison criteria.

The lowest-priced supplier is not automatically the most appropriate choice if the offer introduces unacceptable quality, operational, or security risks.

Employees must disclose relevant personal or financial relationships that could influence supplier selection.

Supplier evaluation records should explain the reasons for selecting a provider when alternatives were considered.

Finance may reject a proposed supplier if required documentation is missing or material risks remain unresolved.

## Technology purchase coordination

Purchases involving software, computing devices, cloud services, or technical infrastructure must be coordinated with the Technology Department.

Technology evaluates compatibility, technical requirements, information security implications, and potential integration dependencies.

Software purchases must comply with the Software Installation and License Management Policy.

Requests for new cloud platforms or external applications may require review of data storage locations, access controls, and vendor security practices.

Technology approval confirms technical suitability but does not replace financial authorization.

Finance must confirm that the purchase has completed all required technical and business reviews before payment commitments are finalized.

Employees must not independently acquire unapproved software subscriptions using personal payment methods to bypass purchasing controls.

## Purchase orders and financial commitments

After approval, Finance or another authorized purchasing function coordinates the formal purchase order when required.

A purchase order identifies the approved goods or services, supplier, quantity, price, and relevant delivery or payment conditions.

Purchase orders provide a documented reference for confirming that supplier invoices correspond to authorized commitments.

Employees must not promise payment, sign contracts, or accept binding supplier terms without appropriate authority.

Changes to approved quantities, prices, or contractual conditions may require additional authorization.

Finance maintains records of approved commitments to support budget monitoring and payment verification.

A purchase request must not be treated as a completed purchase until the required ordering activities have been performed.

## Recurring subscriptions and service agreements

Recurring subscriptions create ongoing financial obligations that may extend beyond the initial purchase date.

Requests for recurring services must identify the billing frequency, renewal conditions, expected users, and anticipated annual cost.

Finance evaluates the total expected commitment rather than considering only the first payment.

Technology may review technical subscriptions to identify duplicated functionality, unused licenses, or compatibility concerns.

Service owners must monitor whether recurring subscriptions continue to provide legitimate business value.

Automatic renewals must be reviewed according to the organization's contract management practices.

Cancellation deadlines and contractual notice requirements should be documented to avoid unnecessary charges.

## Emergency purchases

Emergency purchases may be necessary when an unexpected event threatens essential operations or creates an immediate business risk.

Examples include urgent replacement of critical equipment, emergency infrastructure services, or necessary resources for restoring an unavailable system.

The requester must explain why the purchase cannot follow the normal approval timeline.

Whenever possible, authorization must be obtained from the designated manager and Finance before the financial commitment is made.

If immediate circumstances prevent standard approval, the responsible employee must document the decision and request retrospective review as soon as practical.

Emergency classification does not automatically exempt a purchase from budget, security, or documentation requirements.

Repeated use of emergency purchasing procedures for predictable needs must be reviewed.

## Goods and services receipt confirmation

Receiving an invoice does not automatically confirm that a purchase has been fulfilled.

The requesting department must verify that delivered goods or services correspond to the approved order.

For physical goods, confirmation may include checking quantities, product specifications, and visible damage.

For software or digital services, the responsible department may confirm license activation, account availability, or access to the contracted functionality.

Professional services should be validated against agreed deliverables or milestones.

Discrepancies must be reported before final acceptance whenever possible.

Finance may delay payment processing when receipt confirmation or required supporting documentation is incomplete.

## Invoice verification and payment coordination

Finance verifies supplier invoices against approved purchase records and applicable delivery confirmations.

The review may include supplier identification, invoice amount, currency, tax information, payment terms, and purchase order references.

Differences between an invoice and the approved commitment must be investigated before payment authorization.

Finance must identify potential duplicate invoices and confirm that payments have not already been processed.

Invoices containing unexplained additional charges may require clarification from the supplier or requesting department.

Payment timing follows approved contractual terms and internal financial processing requirements.

Employees must not independently modify supplier banking information or payment instructions outside authorized verification procedures.

## Purchase modifications and cancellations

Business requirements may change after a purchase request has been approved.

Employees must notify the responsible manager and Finance when an approved purchase requires modification or cancellation.

Changes involving additional costs, different suppliers, altered quantities, or new contractual terms may require renewed approval.

Finance evaluates whether the modification affects budget availability or existing commitments.

If an order has already been placed, cancellation may depend on supplier conditions and contractual obligations.

Employees must not assume that an approved request can be transferred to a different product or service without review.

Significant modifications must be documented to preserve an accurate purchasing history.

## Conflict of interest and ethical purchasing

Employees involved in purchasing decisions must act in the legitimate interests of AR Cloud.

Personal relationships, financial incentives, gifts, or external business interests must not improperly influence supplier selection or approval decisions.

Potential conflicts of interest must be disclosed to the appropriate manager or Finance representative.

Employees must not request personal benefits from suppliers in exchange for favorable purchasing decisions.

Confidential supplier quotations and commercial terms must not be shared with unauthorized parties.

Purchasing decisions should be supported by objective business considerations and documented approval records.

Suspected irregularities must be reported through the appropriate internal channels.

## Purchase records and auditability

Finance maintains purchasing records to support financial reporting, accountability, and internal reviews.

Records may include purchase requests, business justifications, quotations, approval decisions, purchase orders, invoices, delivery confirmations, and payment references.

Documents must be stored in approved systems with appropriate access restrictions.

Employees must not intentionally omit relevant information or alter records to conceal unauthorized spending.

Finance may review purchasing records to identify duplicate orders, unusual supplier activity, budget deviations, or incomplete approvals.

Record retention must follow applicable company requirements and relevant legal obligations.

Access to sensitive financial and supplier information must be limited to authorized personnel.

## Roles and responsibilities

Employees identify legitimate purchasing needs and submit accurate requests with sufficient supporting information.

Department managers review business justification, operational necessity, and departmental priorities.

Finance verifies budget availability, purchasing compliance, financial commitments, supplier invoices, and payment authorization.

Technology evaluates technical purchases for compatibility, security, and operational suitability.

System or service owners confirm delivery and acceptance of purchased products or services.

Authorized decision-makers provide additional approval when required by the amount, risk, or nature of the purchase.

All participants must preserve accurate records and avoid unauthorized financial commitments.

## Procedure review and continuous improvement

Finance periodically reviews purchasing activities to identify opportunities for improving efficiency and financial control.

Reviews may consider approval delays, incomplete requests, supplier performance, recurring subscriptions, and unexpected expenditures.

Repeated purchasing problems may result in revised procedures, additional employee guidance, or improved internal controls.

Changes to authorization responsibilities must be communicated to affected departments.

Exceptions to this procedure must be documented and approved by the appropriate financial authority.

This procedure complements the Employee Expense Reimbursement Policy, Technology purchasing requirements, and applicable financial reporting procedures.
