## Purpose and scope

This policy establishes the information security principles that employees, contractors, and authorized third parties must follow when accessing, processing, storing, or sharing company information.

The organization depends on accurate, confidential, and available information to support its daily operations. Unauthorized disclosure, accidental deletion, improper storage, and misuse of company information can affect employees, customers, business partners, and internal services.

The Technology Department is responsible for maintaining technical safeguards, providing security guidance, and coordinating the response to information security incidents. However, every person who handles company information shares responsibility for protecting it.

This policy applies to information in digital and physical formats, regardless of whether it is stored on company systems, approved cloud services, or authorized external platforms.

## Information Classification

Company information must be handled according to its sensitivity and the potential impact of unauthorized disclosure.

The organization uses four information classification levels:

- Public: Information approved for unrestricted external distribution, such as published company announcements, public service descriptions, and official marketing materials.
- Internal: Operational information intended for employees and authorized collaborators, including internal procedures, general project documentation, and routine administrative communications.
- Confidential: Sensitive business information, employee records, customer information, financial reports, contractual documentation, and internal business strategies.
- Restricted: Highly sensitive information requiring additional protection, including authentication secrets, privileged credentials, security configurations, and particularly sensitive personal or business records.

Employees must not assume that information is public simply because it is accessible through a shared company platform.

When the classification of a document is unclear, employees must request clarification from the document owner or the appropriate department before distributing it.

## Information Handling Responsibilities

Employees must access company information only when it is necessary to perform their assigned responsibilities.

Access to a system does not automatically authorize an employee to view, copy, modify, or distribute every document available within that system.

Information owners are responsible for identifying appropriate access restrictions and ensuring that sensitive documents are shared only with authorized recipients.

Employees must verify the intended recipients before sending documents through email, collaboration platforms, or file-sharing services.

Confidential and Restricted information must not be transferred to personal email accounts, unauthorized cloud storage platforms, or unapproved messaging applications.

Printed documents containing sensitive information must not be left unattended in shared offices, meeting rooms, or public areas.

When physical documents are no longer required, employees must follow approved disposal procedures rather than placing sensitive records in ordinary waste containers.

## Credential and Authentication Security

Authentication credentials are personal security assets and must never be shared between employees.

Employees must not disclose passwords, authentication codes, recovery codes, or access tokens through email, chat messages, telephone calls, or informal requests.

Password managers approved by the Technology Department should be used when employees need to manage multiple credentials securely.

Employees must not store passwords in unencrypted documents, shared spreadsheets, browser notes, or publicly accessible repositories.

Multi-factor authentication must be used when required by the organization's account access procedures.

Unexpected authentication prompts must not be approved without verifying that they correspond to a legitimate login attempt.

Employees who suspect that their credentials have been exposed must immediately report the situation to the Technology Department and follow the established account recovery procedure.

Detailed account provisioning, password reset, and permission management processes are defined in the Account Access Procedure.

## Phishing and Social Engineering

Phishing and social engineering attacks attempt to manipulate employees into revealing sensitive information, transferring money, downloading malicious files, or granting unauthorized access.

These attacks may arrive through email, telephone calls, text messages, collaboration tools, or fraudulent websites.

Common warning signs include:

- Unexpected requests for passwords or verification codes.
- Messages creating artificial urgency or threatening disciplinary consequences.
- Requests to bypass normal approval procedures.
- Suspicious attachments or unfamiliar download links.
- Sender addresses that imitate legitimate organizations.
- Unusual requests involving financial information or confidential documents.

Employees must verify suspicious requests using an independent and trusted communication channel.

A message appearing to come from a manager, executive, or Technology Department employee does not automatically make the request legitimate.

Employees must not open suspicious attachments, enter credentials into unfamiliar websites, or follow instructions that conflict with established security procedures.

Suspected phishing attempts must be reported through the organization's approved technology support channel.

## Secure File Storage and Transfer

Company information must be stored in approved systems that provide appropriate access controls, backup capabilities, and administrative oversight.

Employees must avoid creating unnecessary copies of sensitive documents across multiple platforms.

Confidential and Restricted files must be shared using approved services with access permissions appropriate to the information classification.

Public links must not be used to distribute sensitive company information.

Before sharing a document, employees must verify that the recipient requires access and that the sharing permissions do not expose additional files or folders.

Sensitive documents should be protected using encryption when required by the Technology Department or applicable organizational procedures.

Employees must not upload company documents to unapproved external tools, including online file converters, public analysis platforms, or generative artificial intelligence services.

When an external provider requires company information to deliver an authorized service, the information owner and appropriate internal stakeholders must approve the transfer.

## Protection of Personal Information

Personal information belonging to employees, customers, suppliers, and other individuals must be processed only for legitimate and authorized business purposes.

Employees must collect and retain only the personal information necessary to perform the relevant business activity.

Access to personal records must be limited to individuals whose responsibilities require that information.

Personal information must not be copied into development environments, demonstration systems, or testing datasets unless the use has been explicitly authorized and appropriate safeguards are implemented.

Whenever possible, test environments should use synthetic or anonymized information instead of real personal records.

Employees must report accidental disclosure, unauthorized access, or suspected misuse of personal information without unnecessary delay.

Requests involving correction, deletion, disclosure, or retention of personal information must be directed to the department responsible for the applicable process.

## Security Incident Identification and Reporting

An information security incident is an event that compromises, or may compromise, the confidentiality, integrity, or availability of company information.

Examples include unauthorized access, malware infections, credential exposure, accidental information disclosure, lost sensitive documents, and suspicious activity involving company systems.

Employees must report suspected incidents as soon as they become aware of them.

A report should include the approximate time of discovery, affected system or information, observed behavior, and actions already taken.

Employees must not delay reporting because they are uncertain whether the event qualifies as a confirmed security incident.

The Technology Department is responsible for evaluating reported events, determining their severity, and coordinating technical investigation and containment.

Employees must not independently delete evidence, modify security logs, or attempt unauthorized investigations.

If immediate action is necessary to prevent further exposure, employees must follow established emergency instructions or guidance from authorized Technology personnel.

## Remote Work and Public Networks

Employees working outside company facilities must maintain the same information security standards required in the office.

Company information must not be accessed from shared or public computers unless explicitly authorized.

Employees must avoid discussing sensitive business matters in locations where unauthorized individuals can overhear confidential information.

When using public or unfamiliar networks, employees must follow the organization's approved secure connectivity requirements.

Sensitive documents must not be displayed where unrelated individuals can easily view them.

Employees must lock their sessions whenever they leave a workstation unattended, including during remote work.

Remote access permissions, approved connectivity methods, and device requirements are governed by the applicable access and device management procedures.

## Security Awareness and Training

The Technology Department coordinates information security awareness activities to help employees recognize common risks and understand their responsibilities.

Training may address phishing, password protection, information classification, safe file sharing, incident reporting, and emerging security threats.

Employees must participate in mandatory security training when assigned.

Security awareness activities may include simulated phishing exercises, internal communications, practical demonstrations, and updates to existing procedures.

Employees who repeatedly encounter suspicious messages or unusual security events should request additional guidance.

Managers must support employee participation in required training and encourage prompt reporting of potential security concerns.

The purpose of security awareness is to reduce preventable incidents and promote consistent security practices across the organization.

## Compliance and Policy Review

All employees, contractors, and authorized third parties must comply with this policy when handling company information.

Exceptions must be reviewed and approved by the appropriate information owner and authorized Technology personnel before implementation.

The Technology Department periodically reviews this policy to address operational changes, newly identified threats, and modifications to the organization's technical environment.

Significant changes must be communicated to affected employees and departments.

Failure to follow information security requirements may result in corrective actions according to applicable internal procedures.

Employees who need clarification about a security requirement must contact the Technology Department before taking actions that could expose company information.

This policy complements the Account Access Procedure, Company Device Policy, Software Installation Policy, and Technology Support Procedure.
