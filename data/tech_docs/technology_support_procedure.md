# Technology Support and Escalation Procedure

Document ID: TECH-002
Department: Technology
Version: 1.0
Effective date: 2026-01-01
Company: AR Cloud
Document type: Synthetic procedure for an educational project

## Purpose and scope

The Technology support team helps AR Cloud employees with company accounts,
approved applications, managed devices, connectivity, and internal technical
services. This procedure explains how employees submit requests, how support
hours affect response targets, and when an unresolved issue may be escalated.

Technology support does not approve employee benefits, interpret leave
eligibility, authorize expense reimbursements, or decide invoice amounts.
Those decisions belong to HR or Finance. Technology may investigate a
technical failure that prevents an employee from using a system owned by
another department.

This procedure applies to internal employee support. It does not establish
customer support commitments or contractual service levels.

## Support hours and request channels

Regular Technology support operates Monday through Friday, from 09:00 to
18:00 UTC, excluding company holidays. These hours use UTC regardless of the
employee's location. Employees should convert the schedule to their local
time when planning a request or a follow-up.

Employees submit routine requests through the internal Technology support
portal. If an account problem prevents portal access, they use the alternative
support contact published in their onboarding materials. They should provide
a safe contact method and explain that the portal is inaccessible.

Messages sent to an individual technician through an informal chat do not
replace a recorded support request. A technician may help create a ticket,
but employees should obtain the ticket reference before assuming that the
issue has entered the support queue.

The assistant can explain these channels but cannot submit a ticket or
confirm that support has received one.

## Information required in a ticket

A useful ticket identifies the affected application or device, describes the
expected behavior and the actual result, and states when the problem began.
Employees should include the approximate time and timezone, any visible
error message, and whether the issue affects one person or several people.

Employees should also describe the business impact and any deadline affected
by the problem. A deadline helps Technology assess urgency, but it does not
automatically determine the ticket's priority.

Screenshots may be attached after removing confidential information that is
not needed for troubleshooting. Passwords, authentication codes, recovery
codes, API keys, and access tokens must never be included in a ticket.

If the issue concerns a managed device, employees should include its asset
identifier when available. They should not send unrelated personal device
information or complete copies of sensitive business records.

## Priority classification

Technology assigns the final priority after reviewing the available evidence.
Employees describe the impact rather than choosing a priority solely to
obtain a faster response.

A critical incident involves a widespread interruption of a core internal
service with no practical workaround. An example is a company-wide sign-in
failure that prevents multiple teams from working.

A high-priority issue prevents an employee or a team from performing essential
work and has no reasonable workaround. A single employee unable to access
all required work applications may qualify, depending on the verified impact.

A routine issue affects a limited function while work can continue through
an approved workaround. General questions, software installation requests,
and planned access changes normally enter the routine queue.

Suspected security incidents follow the designated security reporting channel.
Employees must not wait for routine ticket classification before reporting
suspected account compromise or an unexpected authentication approval request.

## Initial response targets

During regular support hours, Technology targets an initial response within
one support hour for critical incidents and within four support hours for
high-priority issues. Routine requests have an initial response target of
one working day.

For this procedure, one working day means a day on which regular Technology
support operates. A routine request submitted during support hours should
receive its initial response no later than the same time on the next support
working day.

Support-hour targets count only time inside the published support window.
For example, a high-priority ticket received at 17:00 UTC on Friday has one
support hour available that day. If Monday is not a company holiday, the
remaining three support hours run from 09:00 to 12:00 UTC on Monday.

A request received outside regular hours starts its response clock when the
next support window opens. An automated receipt confirms submission but does
not count as a human initial response.

These targets concern acknowledgement and initial assessment. They do not
guarantee that the issue will be resolved within the same period.

## Requests outside regular hours

Routine employee support is not staffed continuously. Submitting a ticket at
night or during a weekend does not create a guaranteed immediate response.

Employees who observe a critical service interruption outside regular hours
should use the incident reporting channel published in the internal service
directory. That channel alerts the designated incident contact for assessment.
It does not provide general after-hours assistance for routine requests.

Suspected security incidents should be reported immediately through the
designated security channel, including outside regular support hours.
Employees should avoid approving unexpected authentication prompts while
waiting for guidance.

If an employee cannot locate the appropriate incident contact, they should
use the alternative Technology contact in their onboarding materials and
clearly describe the observed impact. This procedure does not publish contact
addresses or promise a specific after-hours response time.

## Follow-up and escalation

Employees should follow up in the existing ticket when its initial response
target has passed without a human response. The follow-up should include
the ticket reference and any change in business impact.

If there is still no response after one additional support working day,
the employee may request review by the Technology support lead through the
support portal or its published alternative contact.

An issue that becomes more disruptive should be updated immediately.
Employees do not need to wait for the original response target to expire
before reporting that additional people or services are affected.

Escalation requests must describe the reason for escalation. Creating several
tickets for the same issue can fragment the investigation and does not
increase priority. Employees should keep updates in the original ticket
unless Technology instructs them to create a separate incident record.

A manager may explain business impact or help coordinate an escalation.
Manager involvement does not override identity verification, access approval,
or security controls.

## Investigation and employee participation

Technology may request additional information, an approved diagnostic step,
or a scheduled troubleshooting session. Employees should respond through
the ticket and follow instructions only when they can verify that the
request comes from an authorized support contact.

Remote assistance requires the employee's awareness and the approved support
tool. Employees must not install an unfamiliar remote-control application
because an unsolicited caller claims to represent Technology.

If support is waiting for information, the ticket should state what is
missing. This procedure does not define an automatic extension of the initial
response target or a universal resolution deadline.

When an approved workaround exists, Technology should explain its limitations.
A workaround that bypasses authentication or exposes confidential information
must not be treated as an acceptable solution.

## Resolution and reopening

Technology records the action taken or the guidance provided before marking
a ticket as resolved. Employees should check whether the reported function
works and whether the original business impact has ended.

If the same problem returns within five working days after resolution,
the employee should request reopening of the original ticket and describe
the recurrence. After that period, the employee should create a new ticket
and reference the earlier case.

A resolved ticket does not prove that every related application or permission
has been reviewed. A separate access request may still be required under
the account access procedure.

The assistant cannot inspect ticket status, assign priority, contact a
technician, or declare that an incident has been resolved. It can explain
the documented procedure and identify the information an employee should
provide.

## Department boundaries

Questions about vacation balances, employee benefits, personal information,
and employment arrangements belong to HR. Questions about reimbursement
eligibility, payment status, and invoice amounts belong to Finance.

A technical failure in an HR or Finance application belongs to Technology
for troubleshooting. The underlying business decision remains with the
department that owns the process.

For example, Technology may investigate an error that prevents an employee
from uploading an expense receipt. Finance decides whether the expense is
eligible and whether the submitted evidence is sufficient.
